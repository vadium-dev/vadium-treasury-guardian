Scenario 2 of 3

The discretionary path

Job 2 · Tier 2/3 evidence · a real judgment call

One vote isn't enough — and the chain proved it

✓ verified on-chain

A 2 USDC job goes to committee. Juror 1 votes to uphold the dispute. Calling executeDiscretionaryVerdict() right then — from any address, including Vadium's own deployer — reverted. Not a UI restriction: the contract itself refused. Only once Juror 2 also voted did quorum exist, and only then did the same slash math as the objective path run.

Proves ADR‑001's core structural claim: a discretionary payout cannot be triggered by a single call — ours or anyone else's — only by a real, on-chain quorum of independent votes.

Scenario 3 of 3

The hedge product

Job 3 · purchased coverage · the insurance-shaped claim

Coverage governs the payout — not the job's price

✓ verified on-chain

This job was only worth 1 USDC. But the buyer had separately purchased 6 USDC of coverage on this seller, paying a 1 USDC premium up front. When the job failed, the buyer's restitution was governed by the coverage amount, not the tiny job price — and the premium had already landed in the shared pool the moment it was paid, win or lose.
